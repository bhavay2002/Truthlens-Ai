Here's a concise breakdown of your repo’s issues and actionable recommendations:


### 1. **Extreme Label Sparsity (Missing Supervision)**
- **Problem:** Most labels for certain tasks are “NA” (not annotated). This isn’t a simple class imbalance—your model essentially has no signal to learn for those labels in most batches.
- **Effects:**  
  - Loss functions become ill-defined or inconsistent (many “empty” targets).
  - Gradient updates become almost random or very weak for those heads/tasks.
  - Heads may essentially stop learning, or learn noise.

#### **How to Fix**
- **a. Improve Label Coverage:**  
  - Annotate more data for the sparse labels. Even a small increase in positive/negative samples for underrepresented tasks can help.
  - If this isn’t possible, consider discarding the most incomplete tasks from multi-task training for now (don’t include task heads without sufficient supervision in batches/epochs).

- **b. Masking & Losses:**  
  - Ensure the loss computation masks out “NA” labels, so only valid (non-NA) samples contribute per task.
  - In multi-task training, only compute gradients for available labels per batch/task.

---

### 2. **Task Dominance (Gradient Imbalance)**
- **Problem:** Well-annotated tasks (e.g. ideology or narrative_frame) produce much stronger gradients than sparser tasks, “drowning out” those tasks during training. The model becomes good at one thing, bad at the others.
- **Effects:**  
  - Some heads never learn.
  - Multi-task claims, but only a single (dominant) task drives training.

#### **How to Fix**
- **a. Gradient Normalization or Balancing:**  
  - Use dynamic loss weighting (e.g. GradNorm, DWA, or uncertainty-based weighting [Kendall et al. 2018]).
  - Explicitly scale down loss for overrepresented tasks, or increase loss for underrepresented ones.

- **b. Curriculum or Alternate Task Sampling:**  
  - Mix batches to include more data for the weaker tasks (over/under-sample, or batch by task).
  - Consider task-specific learning rate schedulers.

---

### 3. **Loss Spikes + Anomaly Flooding**
- **Problem:** Your frequent anomaly/loss spikes are probably due to the above instabilities (missing labels = noisy loss = volatile training).
- **Effects:**  
  - Likely poor, unreliable model convergence.
  - Model may "forget" or overfit erratically.

#### **How to Fix**
- **a. Stabilize Label/Input Pipeline:**  
  - Fix issues listed above first; stable, balanced data = stable loss dynamics.
- **b. Smoothing & Robust Training:**  
  - Try gradient clipping.
  - Use loss smoothing or robust loss functions if class imbalance is extreme.

---

## **Summary of Recommendations**

1. **Increase label coverage** or drop severely incomplete tasks from multi-task setup.
2. **Use loss masking** for NA labels—never average losses with “missing” supervision.
3. **Balance task gradients** via intelligent loss weighting, gradient normalization, or batch balancing.
4. **Monitor batch composition:** Ensure each batch or epoch has sufficient “active” labels for every task.
5. **Use anomaly/loss spike detection** as a signal to adjust your pipeline/settings iteratively.
