## 10. ⚠️ “Newly Initialized Pooler Weights”

**Problem:**  
- `pooler.dense` weights (often in transformers like BERT/Roberta) are newly reinitialized instead of loaded from pretrained checkpoints.
- Impact:  
  - CLS pooling may be noisy/unstable at first training steps.
  - Usually minor in the long run, but can slow convergence or degrade performance for classification.

**Fix:**  
- Always try to load all weights, including pooler, from the pretrained base model.
- If you must reinit (e.g., for new head architecture), consider using a smaller learning rate for pooler/later layers.
- Log a warning when pooler/init weights are not from checkpoint, to catch this early in new experiments.

---

## 11. ⚠️ Over-aggressive Anomaly Logging

**Problem:**  
- Too many anomaly logs/debug dumps (e.g., `spike_*.pt`), especially for every loss spike.
- Impact:  
  - Slows down training.
  - Consumes disk space rapidly.
  - Signal lost in the noise of logs.

**Fix:**  
- Log anomalies sparingly:
    - Limit saving to 1 in every N spikes (e.g., only major spikes or intervals).
    - Set a max number or rotate saved anomaly files.
    - Summarize spike stats in logs, save details only for outliers.
- Optionally, use a rolling buffer (only last K anomaly logs kept).

---

## 12. ⚠️ Gradient Norm Always ~1.0

**Problem:**  
- Gradient norm per step is always ≈1.0.
- Highly suspicious: likely aggressive gradient clipping or normalization.
- **Impact:**  
  - Can “hide” instability: model might be flipping between extremes, but norms appear flat due to rescaling.
  - Loss may look stable while gradients are not informative.

**Fix:**  
- Audit your optimizer/training scripts:
    - Check for `torch.nn.utils.clip_grad_norm_` or equivalent — review threshold policy.
    - If using per-parameter normalization, log unclipped/pre-clipped gradient norms for diagnosis.
- Only apply strong clipping if genuinely necessary for explosion, not as a crutch for batch/model issues.
- Review gradient statistics before and after clipping—not just final values.
