## 7. ⚠️ No Evidence of Task Weighting

**Problem:**  
 - Equal loss weighting across tasks when label availability is sparse (and task difficulty varies) leads to broken optimization.
 - The model overfits the most common/easy task, while harder or sparser tasks receive little meaningful gradient.

**Fixes:**
- **Implement Dynamic Task Weighting:**  
  - Use established methods like:
    - **GradNorm** (dynamically normalizes gradients across tasks)
    - **Uncertainty-based Weighting** ([Kendall et al., 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.07115))
    - **DWA (Dynamic Weight Averaging)**
    - **PCGrad** ([Yu et al., 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.06782))
  - Track and log weight values per batch/epoch for transparency.

**PyTorch Example (Uncertainty Weighting Skeleton):**
```python name=uncertainty_weighting.py
# sigma_t are learnable log variances per task
loss = sum((1/(2*sigma_t**2))*task_loss + log(sigma_t) for task_loss, sigma_t in zip(task_losses, log_vars))
```
- Start with uncertainty weighting (plug-in ready), then try GradNorm for more advanced balancing.

## 8. ⚠️ Harder Tasks Dominate Due to Larger Gradients

**Problem:**  
 - Ideology task has systematically higher loss/gradients (1.1–1.2) vs. others (0.6–0.7).
 - If losses are directly summed, the harder/more difficult task produces larger gradients and overwhelms the shared encoder.

**Fixes:**
- **Loss Scaling/Normalization:**  
  - Scale per-task losses so that their average magnitude is more comparable.
  - Use normalization techniques (like dividing loss by per-task running mean or standard deviation) before summation.
- **Apply dynamic weights as above** (point 7).

**Extra Tip:**  
- **Log gradient norms per task** during training to verify which heads are dominating.
- Optionally, **clip or rescale gradients per task** before aggregation.

## 9. ⚠️ Misleading Training Health Metric

**Problem:**  
 - “Training health” = 1.00 is not aligned with actual model learning.
 - Despite task dominance and loss spikes, the health metric is “healthy”—likely because it doesn’t account for the balance/consistency of all tasks.

**Fixes:**
- **Design Task-aware Health Metrics:**
  - Include:
    - Task loss variance over time (should not spike).
    - Ratio of active updates per head.
    - Task gradient norms balance over training.
    - Coverage of each task in recent batches.
- **Example:**  
    - Report “% of batches with at least X tasks active”
    - Plot rolling average of per-task losses on TensorBoard/WandB
    - Compute and track “gradient fairness” metrics (see GradNorm/PCGrad papers)
